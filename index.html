What to expect when quiting vaping

A clear guide to what quitting looks like. Learn what to expect, how to handle cravings, and where to find support along the way.

01

Make a quit plan

  • Pick a quit date — close enough to feel real, far enough to prepare.
  • Write down why you want to quit. Keep it somewhere you'll actually see it.
  • Decide whether you'll quit all at once or taper down gradually.
  • Identify your biggest triggers so they don't catch you off guard.
02

Know what withdrawal can feel like

  • Cravings
  • Irritability or mood changes
  • Trouble concentrating
  • Restlessness
  • Changes in sleep or appetite
These symptoms are common and usually temporary — most people find they ease with time.
03

Have a craving plan

Something to actually do when a craving hits:

  • Delay — wait a few minutes, the urge will pass.
  • Drink water.
  • Take slow breaths.
  • Go for a short walk or do something physical.
  • Keep your hands or mouth busy.
  • Distract yourself or reach out to someone supportive.

The craving toolkit below walks through each of these in more depth.

04

Identify and avoid your triggers

Common ones include:

  • Stress
  • Alcohol or parties
  • Driving
  • Studying
  • Certain friends or social situations
  • Seeing or smelling someone else vaping
05

Get support

Tell friends or family you're quitting. Talk with a healthcare professional. Use a quitline or text-based program. Quitting is easier when you're not doing it alone.

06

Nicotine replacement and medications

Some people benefit from nicotine replacement therapy or prescription medications as part of quitting. This is health information, not a recommendation — talk with a healthcare professional about what's appropriate for you.

See a reputable source like smokefree.gov or your doctor before starting anything.
07

What to do after a slip

Vaping once doesn't mean you've failed. Figure out what triggered the slip, learn from it, and restart your quit attempt. Most people who quit successfully had at least one slip along the way.

08

A simple day-by-day plan

  • Before quitting — set your date, tell your support people, clear out your vapes and supplies.
  • Quit day — remove temptation, keep busy, use your craving plan.
  • First week — expect withdrawal to be at its toughest here. Lean on your support and toolkit.
  • Weeks 2–4 — cravings usually get shorter and less frequent. Keep track of what triggers you.
  • Staying vape-free — stay alert to triggers, and have a plan ready if a craving catches you off guard.
09

Motivation and benefits

People quit for their own mix of reasons — health, money, freedom from cravings, not having to carry a device everywhere. Keep your own reasons close; they'll matter most on the hard days.

10

Emergency and high-risk situations

If you experience concerning symptoms, seek medical help.

This site is a support tool, please do not use this website for all of your medical information.

When it hits

Five things to do instead of vaping

If a craving occurs, try doing one of these methods.

~1

Box breathing

Four counts in, hold, out, hold. Repeat until the pull drops.

~2

Cold water

Splash your face or hold an ice cube. The sudden cold interrupts the craving signal in your brain.

~3

Text someone

Message one person who knows you're quitting. Just "craving right now" is enough. Don't do this one alone.

~4

Move your body

Ten push-ups, a lap around the block, stairs. Physical movement burns off the same restlessness nicotine used to.

~5

Chew something

Gum, a straw, sunflower seeds. Give your mouth and hands a job so they're not reaching for the device.

~6

Name the craving

Say out loud what's happening: "This is a craving. It will peak and pass." Naming it takes some of its power away.

What you're earning

Milestones worth marking

Recovery starts faster than most people expect. These are the badges most people unlock after their last vape — your timeline may vary.

🌱
20 minutes

First step

Heart rate and blood pressure start settling back toward your normal range.

🌤️
12 hours

Clearer air

Oxygen carrying capacity in your blood improves as carbon monoxide clears out.

💪
48–72 hours

Toughest stretch, cleared

Nicotine is fully out of your system. Cravings often peak here before they start to ease.

👃
1–2 weeks

Senses sharpening

Circulation improves, and taste and smell — often dulled by regular use — start coming back.

🫁
1 month

Breathing easier

Lung function keeps improving as coughing or shortness of breath continues to fade.

🏆
1 year

Rare urge

Most people find cravings show up far less often, and far less intensely, than they did early on.
