When it hits

Five things to do instead of vaping

If a craving occurs, try doing one of these methods.

~1

Box breathing

Use the tool at the top of this page. Four counts in, hold, out, hold. Repeat until the pull drops.

~2

Cold water

Splash your face or hold an ice cube. The sudden cold interrupts the craving signal in your brain.

~3

Text someone

Message one person who knows you're quitting. Just "craving right now" is enough. Don't do this one alone.

~4

Move your body

Ten push-ups, a lap around the block, stairs. Physical movement burns off the same restlessness nicotine used to.

~5

Chew something

Gum, a straw, sunflower seeds. Give your mouth and hands a job so they're not reaching for the device.

~6

Name the craving

Say out loud what's happening: "This is a craving. It will peak and pass." Naming it takes some of its power away.

What's happening in your body

Recovery starts faster than most people expect

This is the general pattern most people experience after their last vape. Your timeline may vary.

20 minutes

Heart rate begins to settle

The nicotine-driven spike in heart rate and blood pressure starts coming back down toward your normal range.

12 hours

Carbon monoxide levels normalize

Oxygen carrying capacity in your blood improves as carbon monoxide from inhaled vapor clears out.

48–72 hours

Nicotine is fully out of your system

This is often the hardest stretch — cravings and irritability tend to peak here before they start to ease.

1–2 weeks

Circulation and taste sharpen

Blood flow improves, and your senses of taste and smell — often dulled by regular use — start coming back.

1 month

Breathing feels easier

Lung function keeps improving and coughing or shortness of breath from irritated airways continues to fade.

1 year

Cravings become rare

Most people find the urge shows up far less often, and far less intensely, than it did in the first weeks.
